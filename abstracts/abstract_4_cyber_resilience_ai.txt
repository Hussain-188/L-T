Title: CyberSentinel — An AI-Driven Adaptive Threat Detection and Response System with Human-in-the-Loop Governance

Abstract:

Security operations centers today face a paradox of volume: intrusion detection systems, endpoint agents, and network monitors collectively generate thousands of alerts per day, yet the vast majority are false positives or low-priority duplicates. Analysts spend the bulk of their time sifting through noise rather than investigating genuine threats, and by the time a true positive is identified, an attacker may have already moved laterally across the environment. CyberSentinel is a proof-of-concept system that combines machine learning detection, agentic alert triage, and governed automated response to compress the time between the first suspicious signal and effective containment — while keeping a human firmly in control of every high-impact action.

The system operates across four coordinated layers. An ingestion layer consumes raw network traffic records and endpoint telemetry, engineers discriminative features — flow statistics, payload entropy, behavioral ratios, and temporal patterns — and feeds them to an ensemble detection engine comprising gradient-boosted classifiers for known attack signatures and an autoencoder-based anomaly detector for novel threat patterns. Alerts produced by the detection layer are forwarded to an AI triage agent that clusters related alerts into coherent incident narratives, estimates severity, maps observed tactics to the MITRE ATT&CK framework, and recommends a prioritized sequence of response actions. For low-risk containment steps such as blocking a single external address, the agent may act autonomously within predefined policy guardrails. For high-impact interventions — isolating a server, disabling a user account, triggering a forensic capture — the system surfaces a structured approval request to a human analyst through a dedicated escalation interface, presenting the supporting evidence and the agent's reasoning before any action is taken.

Evaluation will be conducted on the publicly available CIC-IDS benchmark dataset, measuring detection recall, false positive rate, mean time to detect, and mean time to respond against a rule-based baseline, with all detections mapped to standardized ATT&CK technique identifiers.
